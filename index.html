Tasting menu

The menu
changes nightly.

Our approach

Below — the feelings
that stay.

There is no fixed menu at Noir. Each evening, the chef composes a new sequence of twelve courses built entirely around what the season offers and what the moment demands. No two evenings are the same.

I
Act one
Cold Opening
Lightness, acidity, first contact. Three or four small forms arrive in quick succession — the palate waking up, the evening beginning. No introductions. Just presence.
3–4 courses · cold preparations
II
Act two
Between Two Seasons
Contrast and tension. This is the most unpredictable part of the evening — where the kitchen plays with temperature, texture, and expectation. Something familiar arriving in an unfamiliar form.
2–3 courses · warm and cold
III
Act three
The Main Thought
The heart of the evening. Depth, warmth, the chef's signature. This is where a single ingredient — a root, a fish, a cut of meat — is given its full say. Nothing ornamental. Only essence.
2–3 courses · main compositions
IV
Act four
The Ending
Sweet, quiet, final. Not a sugar rush — a resolution. Two or three moments of sweetness that echo something from earlier in the evening. The conversation closes on its own terms.
2–3 courses · desserts & mignardises

The chef

Maxim
Koval

Once studied physics. Then understood that food is a more precise science — where every variable matters, and every result is unrepeatable.

A path without a fixed destination

The journey

Kyiv

The beginning

Physics, then kitchens

A degree started, then abandoned. The first kitchen job taken not for passion but for money — and the discovery that precision meant something entirely different here.

Copenhagen

Stage

Learning restraint

An internship in one of the north's most precise kitchens. Where he learned that subtraction is a technique — that removing something is as deliberate as adding it.

Tokyo

Two years

A different relationship with ingredients

Japan changed everything about how he saw a product. Not as raw material to transform, but as something with its own voice — worth listening to before deciding anything.

Kyiv

Now

Noir

Returned with no interest in a fixed menu or a national cuisine. Built Noir around a single question: what can this ingredient say, today, in this season?

Philosophy

Building menus
around questions.

I don't build menus around a country or an era. I build them around a question: what can this ingredient say today? The answer changes with the season, the market, the mood — and that's the point.

No two evenings at Noir are the same. That's not a limitation. That's the entire idea.

In his own words

"I don't cook dishes. I capture a moment — this season, this ingredient, this state of mind."

— Maxim Koval, Chef and founder, Noir
